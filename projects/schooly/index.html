Schooly: from campus evidence to community action

A community-guided decision-support prototype that helps K–12 school teams see where shade reaches student-use areas, compare planting choices, and record human-reviewed decisions.

The question

How can a school team see whether shade actually reaches the ground students use, compare practical planting choices, and decide together what to do next?

Approach

  1. Separate canopy stock (how much tree cover a campus has) from functional shade (where and when shade reaches student-use ground).
  2. Compute shade on an illustrative campus in the browser from explicit sun geometry, keeping tree shade and building shade separate.
  3. Pair scenario comparisons with community input and a human approval step; nothing is acted on automatically.

Current checkpoint

The live demo runs a simplified geometric shadow calculation on an illustrative campus. It is not a SOLWEIG, mean radiant temperature or UTCI simulation, and it shows no data from any real school. The approach builds on the author's New Mexico schoolyard shade research, which is an unpublished working manuscript.

Scope & interpretation. Illustrative geometry, simplified crown shapes, one summer date and clear-sky sun positions. Modeled shade is not thermal comfort, and demo results are not predictions for any particular school.

Next questions

  • Add the community-feedback and decision-brief steps to the demo.
  • Review which New Mexico research outputs can be cleared for public display.
  • Test the workflow with school teams before claiming practical use.
