From urban geometry to radiant exposure

A reproducible workflow direction for pedestrian-level radiant-heat modeling with SOLWEIG, GIS, urban morphology, and meteorological data.

→
SOLWEIG scenario workflow
→
Shade + mean radiant temperature

The question

How can consistent geometry and weather inputs support interpretable comparisons of pedestrian radiant exposure?

Approach

  1. Prepare compatible terrain, building, vegetation, and sky-view inputs.
  2. Organize meteorological forcing and model parameters reproducibly.
  3. Plan comparable scenarios for shade and mean radiant temperature.

Current checkpoint

The available repository description defines the workflow objective. The Clark Kerr pilot provides related geometry work, but no campus heatwave simulation is claimed at this checkpoint.

Scope & interpretation. Modeled radiant temperature, observed air temperature, and thermal-comfort indices are distinct quantities. Their inputs and validation needs differ.

Next questions

  • Verify geometry, vegetation, and meteorological conventions.
  • Run a small modeling pilot before interpreting event-scale scenario differences.
