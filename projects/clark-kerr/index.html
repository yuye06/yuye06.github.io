Clark Kerr: a campus in 3D

Combining airborne and portable LiDAR to understand campus geometry and prepare for pedestrian radiant-heat modeling.

Open the interactive 3D explorer ↗

The question

What does a portable survey add to an airborne representation of a campus, and where is that geometry reliable enough to inform heat-exposure analysis?

Approach

  1. Audit USGS airborne tiles and reconstruct a portable LiDAR recording.
  2. Bring the point clouds into a shared horizontal and vertical reference.
  3. Inspect agreement on spatially held-out surfaces and keep uncertainty explicit.
  4. Build toward comparable geometry inputs for SOLWEIG radiant-heat scenarios.

Current checkpoint

The current explorer includes four primary airborne tiles, two optional overlapping versions, and the diagnostic registered portable cloud. All six unique airborne files were fully decoded. The primary tile footprints span the provisional campus and its 100 m buffer; observed surface coverage can still vary.

Scope & interpretation. The portable transform comes from the original northern pilot. Median absolute held-out disagreement there was 0.173 m, with a 1.444 m 95th percentile. Those checks do not establish survey accuracy or validate the newly added area. Analytical fusion and heatwave modeling remain pending.

Next questions

  • Inspect local discrepancies and qualify any portable contributions before analytical fusion.
  • Review the campus boundary and compare building and canopy products.
  • Prepare a consistent geometry stack and documented event-specific meteorology.
